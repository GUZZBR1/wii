# Auto-calibration — tolerance for transient tracking loss during the collection window

Date: November 25, 2026 · Branch: `fix/autocalibrator-lost-tolerance` (commit `46983f9`) · Issue: [santleme/nevergear#9](https://github.com/santleme/nevergear/issues/9)

Research based on **primary sources**: the BlazePose paper (arXiv 2006.10204, CVPRW 2020), the C++ graph code in the `google-ai-edge/mediapipe` GitHub repository (the `master` branch as read on this date), official protos, `vision.d.ts`/`pose_landmarker.ts` from tasks-vision, official documentation (ai.google.dev, developers.google.com/ml-kit, the Azure Kinect Body Tracking SDK reference, and the legacy solution at mediapipe.readthedocs.io), and repository code. Complements `docs/auditoria-mediapipe.md` (internal smoothing and thresholds) and `docs/pesquisa-pipeline-cv.md` (per-frame pipeline).

**Question**: During a fixed-duration sample-collection window, what is the correct model for tolerating transient tracking loss — abort immediately on the first `lost` state, or absorb the loss into the coverage denominator and let deadline + coverage determine the outcome?

---

## 1. Context

`AutoCalibrator` (`src/cv/autoCalibrator.ts`) calibrates in four phases: `framing` (required body visible) → `settling` (body still for `SETTLE_MS = 1000`, `:5`) → `collecting` (`COLLECT_MS = 3000`, `:6`) → `done`. `TrackingGate` decides who advances or regresses state (`src/kinematics/trackingGate.ts:26-35`): `tracked` after 3 consecutive good frames, `lost` after 4 consecutive bad frames — `min(visibility, presence)` < 0.55 on the 4 central points [11,12,23,24] —, and `degraded` between the two. Four frames at 30 fps ≈ **~133 ms** — the loss-detection window is short by design.

The issue’s asymmetry: during `collecting`, a `degraded` frame or a frame out of view was already tolerated — it entered the sample denominator and the deadline clock (`Calibrator.finish()` requires ≥12 samples, span ≥2800 ms, and `valid.length ≥ samples.length × 0.65`, `src/cv/calibrator.ts:23-26,76`). But the 4th bad frame — state `lost` — called `reset()` and discarded the entire attempt (~3 s of samples), restarting from `framing`. The fix proposed in the issue and implemented in commit `46983f9`: treat `lost` during `collecting` as `degraded` (`autoCalibrator.ts:27-43`), while keeping the reset when collection has not yet started.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 What `visibility` and `presence` mean (and how they are produced)

1. **`visibility` = “the point is visible (not occluded by other objects)”; a point outside the scene bounds also counts as invisible. `presence` = “the point is present in the scene (within the image bounds).”** Proto definition: `landmark.proto` fields 4 and 5 — “Float score of whether landmark is visible or occluded by other objects. Landmark considered as invisible also if it is not present on the screen (out of scene bounds)” / “Float score of whether landmark is present on the scene (located within scene bounds).” Both may be emitted as “sigmoid or argument of sigmoid,” **but in this graph they are probabilities**: `ConfigureTensorsToLandmarksCalculator` applies `SIGMOID` to `visibility_activation` and `presence_activation` for normalized landmarks (`pose_landmarks_detector_graph.cc` ~L145-160). `worldLandmarks` receive the same copied scores through `VisibilityCopyCalculator` (~L471-478). Sources: [landmark.proto](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/framework/formats/landmark.proto) L25-44, L57-63; `vision.d.ts:2187-2193,2476-2482` (“likelihood of the landmark being visible within the image” / “present in the scene (within image bounds)”).

2. **`visibility` is temporally smoothed by an EMA with α = 0.1; `presence` is not smoothed — it is emitted raw per frame.** With `runningMode: VIDEO` + `numPoses: 1` (our configuration, `pose.worker.ts:16-24`), the graph enables `smooth_landmarks` and applies `SmoothLandmarksVisibility(low_pass_filter_alpha = 0.1)` to per-frame landmark lists (`pose_landmarks_detector_graph.cc` ~L774-816). The filter is `LowPassVisibilityFilter`: `result = alpha·new + (1−alpha)·previous` (`low_pass_filter.cc:25-35`), applied **only** to the `visibility` field — `*out_landmark = in_landmark` copies the rest unchanged, including `presence` (`visibility_smoothing_calculator.cc` ~L97-118). Numerical consequence: starting near 0.9, emitted visibility crosses 0.55 only after **~5 consecutive frames** with a raw value ≈ 0 (0.9·0.9ⁿ); on recovery, it takes **~7 frames** to rise from ~0.1 to 0.55 even if the person is already visible. Oscillations of 1–3 frames do not collapse the field. Sources: `pose_landmarks_detector_graph.cc` ~L774-816; `visibility_smoothing_calculator.cc` ~L97-118; `low_pass_filter.cc:25-35`. (Finding #1 in `docs/auditoria-mediapipe.md` said “on `visibility`/`presence`” — corrected here: only `visibility` receives the EMA.)

3. **Coordinates are also smoothed (internal One Euro filter)** — `min_cutoff=0.05, beta=80` for normalized coordinates, `0.1/40` for world coordinates (code comments: α≈0.01–0.02 when still, α≈0.8–0.94 during fast movement). In other words, during brief occlusion the model continues emitting damped, plausible coordinates — the cost appears in the scores, not as a position jump. Source: `pose_landmarks_detector_graph.cc` ~L780-816; finding #1 in `docs/auditoria-mediapipe.md`.

### 2.2 How the landmarker’s own tracking behaves between frames

4. **The pipeline is detector→tracker with a per-frame ROI loop — not “detection every frame” and not internal hysteresis.** The graph feeds `POSE_RECTS_NEXT_FRAME` (a rectangle derived from the previous frame’s 2 auxiliary keypoints) back through `PreviousLoopbackCalculator`; `NormalizedRectVectorHasMinSizeCalculator` emits `has_enough_poses`, and `DisallowIf(image_in, has_enough_poses)` **skips the detector** when there was an ROI in the previous frame. The file’s own comment says: “PoseLandmarkerGraph tracks the landmarks over time, and skips the PoseDetectorGraph. If the tracking is lost or the detected poses are less than configured max number poses, PoseDetectorGraph would be triggered to detect poses.” Source: [pose_landmarker_graph.cc](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/tasks/cc/vision/pose_landmarker/pose_landmarker_graph.cc) ~L168-172 (comment), ~L318-324 (`set_smooth_landmarks` for `num_poses==1`), ~L354-379 (`PreviousLoopback`/`has_enough_poses`/`DisallowIf`/`AssociationNormRectCalculator.min_similarity_threshold = min_tracking_confidence`).

5. **When the pose “fails” in a frame, the detector runs on the next frame — redetection is per-frame, with no waiting window.** A per-ROI scalar controls emission: model `pose_flag` → `TensorsToFloatsCalculator` → `pose_presence_score` → `ThresholdingCalculator(threshold = min_detection_confidence)` → `pose_presence` boolean feeding the `ALLOW` input of a `GateCalculator` that holds all output tensors (landmarks, segmentation, heatmap, world). `pose_presence = false` → **nothing is emitted in that frame**: neither landmarks nor `pose_rect_next_frame` → `has_enough_poses = false` → the next frame runs the full-frame detector again. This subgraph’s `min_detection_confidence` is what JavaScript exposes as **`minPosePresenceConfidence`** (`pose_landmarker.ts` `setOptions`: `minPoseDetectionConfidence` → `poseDetectorGraphOptions`; `minPosePresenceConfidence` → `poseLandmarksDetectorGraphOptions.setMinDetectionConfidence`; `minTrackingConfidence` → parent graph options). Sources: `pose_landmarks_detector_graph.cc` ~L384-405 (threshold + gate), ~L555-580 (next-frame rect comes from auxiliary landmarks — and therefore depends on the gated tensors); [pose_landmarker.ts](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/tasks/web/vision/pose_landmarker/pose_landmarker.ts) ~L208-224; `docs/pesquisa-pipeline-cv.md` §6.5 (`minTrackingConfidence` is inert with `numPoses=1`).

6. **The app’s loss state is coarser than the graph’s loss.** `TrackingGate` requires 4 consecutive bad frames for `lost`; every “empty” frame (flag < 0.5) or low-confidence frame counts. The graph, in turn, retries detection on every subsequent frame — when the person reappears, reacquisition happens on the first good frame, and the app’s ~4 `lost` frames measure **sustained** loss, not redetection latency. Source: `pose_landmarker_graph.cc` ~L354-379; `src/kinematics/trackingGate.ts:26-35`.

7. **BlazePose is designed to “predict through occlusion and mark the score” — it was trained for this.** Paper §2.6: “To support the prediction of invisible points, we simulate occlusions (random rectangles filled with various colors) during training and introduce a per-point visibility classifier that indicates whether a particular point is occluded and if the position prediction is deemed inaccurate. **This allows tracking a person constantly even for cases of significant occlusions, like upper body-only or when the majority of person body is out of scene**.” And §2.1: “The tracker predicts keypoint coordinates, the presence of the person on the current frame, and the refined region of interest... **When the tracker indicates that there is no human present, we re-run the detector network on the next frame.**” Source: [arXiv 2006.10204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10204) §2.1, §2.6; [Google Research blog, Aug. 13, 2020](https://research.google/blog/on-device-real-time-body-pose-tracking-with-mediapipe-blazepose/) (“the detector is run only on the first frame. For subsequent frames we derive the ROI from the previous frame's pose keypoints”).

### 2.3 What reaches the app in a bad frame

8. **A frame with no pose ⇒ empty arrays; a frame with a pose ⇒ always 33 points + scores.** In JavaScript, `result.landmarks` is an empty outer `[[]]` when nothing is emitted; the worker runs `simplify(result.landmarks[0])` → `[]` (`pose.worker.ts:81-86,28-30`). A `POSE` reply returns for **every** requested frame — the calibrator deadline clock (`snapshot.timestampMs`) keeps moving during loss. There is one emission gate for both landmarks and worldLandmarks (they are emitted together or not at all — `tensors_gate` outputs 0 and 3). Source: `src/cv/pose.worker.ts:28-30,81-86`; `src/cv/poseClient.ts:140-146`; `pose_landmarks_detector_graph.cc` ~L398-405.

9. **`lost` ⟹ `!visible` in `AutoCalibrator` — `lost` frames are a strict subset of frames already tolerated.** `lost` requires the current frame to be bad at all 4 central points (missing or `min(vis,presence)` < 0.55 — `trackingGate.ts:22-35`, `math.ts:14-21`); `visible` requires all 8 points (or 10 for running) to be present and ≥0.55 (`autoCalibrator.ts:25`). Since the required core points are a subset, every `lost` frame also fails `visible` — during collection it enters the same tolerated branch already used for `degraded`/invisible frames. Source: `src/kinematics/trackingGate.ts`, `src/cv/autoCalibrator.ts:25-43`.

### 2.4 First-hand precedents for sampling windows under occlusion

10. **Azure Kinect Body Tracking SDK emits an occluded joint as a “predicted joint pose” with a confidence level, not as absence/abort.** `k4abt_joint_confidence_level_t`: `K4ABT_JOINT_CONFIDENCE_LOW` = “The joint is not observed (likely due to occlusion), **predicted joint pose**”; `NONE` = out of range. The official sample renders LOW joints in a muted color — the consumer decides based on confidence, frame by frame. Source: [official SDK reference](https://microsoft.github.io/Azure-Kinect-Body-Tracking/release/1.x.x/group__btstructures_ga8582ddc16801cb72d4d3691bcfc3cddb.html); [simple_3d_viewer/main.cpp](https://github.com/microsoft/Azure-Kinect-Samples/blob/master/body-tracking-samples/simple_3d_viewer/main.cpp) (filter `confidence_level >= K4ABT_JOINT_CONFIDENCE_LOW`).

11. **ML Kit Pose Detection continues to emit unrecognized landmarks with coordinates outside the image + per-point `InFrameLikelihood`** — “it also detects a partial body pose. In that case the landmarks that are not recognized are assigned coordinates outside of the image”; `InFrameLikelihood` is “the probability that the landmark is within the image frame.” Same contract: best effort + per-sample score, aggregated by the consumer. Source: [developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/pose-detection](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/pose-detection).

12. **MediaPipe’s official pose-classification guide prescribes absorbing frame-to-frame noise with EMA + double-threshold hysteresis — not aborting.** “we apply exponential moving average (EMA) smoothing to level any noise from pose prediction or classification”; for repetition counting, “the threshold used to detect when the state is exited is actually slightly lower than the one used to detect when the state is entered. It creates an interval where the pose class and the counter can't be changed” — to avoid ghost counts when the signal oscillates around the threshold. The ML Kit sample implements this as `EMASmoothing` (10-result window, α=0.2; resets only when the *interval between inputs* exceeds 100 ms — `RESET_THRESHOLD_MS`, i.e. reset on a stream stall, not on a bad frame). Sources: [pose_classification.md](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/3eb8983cde1517fdff4abbfb33586316af6d45be/docs/solutions/pose_classification.md) (repetition counting; file removed from `master` — link pinned to the last commit containing it); [googlesamples/mlkit EMASmoothing.java](https://github.com/googlesamples/mlkit/blob/master/android/vision-quickstart/app/src/main/java/com/google/mlkit/vision/demo/java/posedetector/classification/EMASmoothing.java) `DEFAULT_WINDOW_SIZE=10`, `RESET_THRESHOLD_MS=100`.

13. **The legacy solution documents the same per-frame recovery invariant**: “for video use cases the detector is invoked only as needed, i.e., for the very first frame and when the tracker could no longer identify body pose presence in the previous frame.” Source: [mediapipe.readthedocs.io/en/latest/solutions/pose.html](https://mediapipe.readthedocs.io/en/latest/solutions/pose.html) (ML Pipeline).

### 2.5 Window arithmetic

14. **65% coverage tolerates ~1 s of continuous loss — ~8× the `lost` detection time (~133 ms).** A 3-second window at ~30 fps gives ~90 samples; `valid ≥ 0.65·N` allows ~31 bad samples ≈ **1.03 s** of continuous blackout, or any distribution of partial losses totaling ≤35%. Allowing ~6–10 recovery frames afterward (the visibility EMA takes ~7 frames to rise above 0.55 — evidence 2) still leaves room for real losses of ~1 s or more. Source: `src/cv/calibrator.ts:23-26,76`; `src/cv/autoCalibrator.ts:5-6`; evidence 2.

## 3. Answer to the question

**Absorb loss into the coverage denominator + deadline — no primary source supports immediate abort.**

- The framework itself is built around “absorb and recover”: the model predicts through occlusions and marks low scores (evidence 7); the graph emits nothing for a bad frame but retries detection on the next one (evidence 4–6); and the output carries smoothed/raw scores for the consumer to filter (evidence 1–3).
- Every first-hand SDK/document follows the same contract — emit best effort per sample + confidence, then aggregate in the consumer: predicted joints with `CONFIDENCE_LOW` (Azure Kinect, evidence 10), coordinates outside the image + `InFrameLikelihood` (ML Kit, evidence 11). No official document recommends discarding a sampling window at the first bad frame; the prescribed pattern for per-frame signals is EMA + hysteresis (evidence 12) — exactly what the existing pair `TrackingGate` (3-good/4-bad hysteresis) + `Calibrator` (coverage aggregation + median) already implements.
- From a correctness perspective, `lost` carries no new information during collection: every `lost` frame is necessarily `!visible` (evidence 9) and would be rejected by `Calibrator`’s per-sample filter anyway (`calibrator.ts:58-60`) — immediate `reset()` only destroyed the good samples around it.
- The only thing immediate abort bought was retry latency: by absorbing loss, an attempt can take up to ~3 s longer to fail (when loss covers >35% of the window). But failures like that already cost the entire reframing period (~4 s) under the old model — the expected result is to save attempts that coverage would approve, which is the common case for ~130–300 ms blackouts.

**Verdict: the evidence supports the fix proposed in the issue — and the implementation on the branch (absorb only during `collecting`, keep reset before it) is consistent with the model of “protecting accumulated investment.”**

## 4. Fix implications — edge cases to cover

1. **`lost` snapshots may have `landmarks: []`/`worldLandmarks: []`** (presence flag <0.5 → graph emits nothing — evidence 5, 8). In the tolerance branch, `update` does not index the points (`visible` is already false because `every` short-circuits, `autoCalibrator.ts:25`); `calibrator.add()` only appends the snapshot, and `finish()` skips samples without the points (`calibrator.ts:46-58`). They count in the denominator — desired behavior, covered by the branch test (`lostPose` with empty arrays).
2. **`lost` snapshots with landmarks present** (pose flag ≥0.5 but central points <0.55 — heavy occlusion where the model still “sees” the person): coordinates are model predictions. `finish()` filters by `bodyConfidence ≥0.55` using a set that includes all 4 central points — same point family and threshold as the gate — so no frame rejected by the gate becomes a valid sample. There is no path for median contamination through `lost`. (`calibrator.ts:58-60` vs. `trackingGate.ts:22-26`, `math.ts:14-21`.)
3. **The deadline continues to be met as frames arrive — including `lost` frames.** `progress` uses `snapshot.timestampMs` (`autoCalibrator.ts:39`), and `conclude()` fires on a `lost` frame too — the attempt ends at the deadline and coverage determines acceptance. Covered by the test “ends the attempt on time even when tracking does not return.”
4. **The smoothing tail counts as additional loss.** After the person returns, emitted `visibility` takes ~7 frames to rise above 0.55 (evidence 2); these recovery frames enter the denominator as invalid (~230 ms extra per burst). The arithmetic in section 2, item 14 already accounts for this — but it explains why “130 ms of loss” at the beginning becomes ~350–400 ms of invalid samples by the end of recovery.
5. **Raw `presence` → instantaneous dips, but only `degraded`.** A point leaving the frame lowers `presence` in the same frame (no smoothing — evidence 2): this produces absorbed `degraded`/invisible frames, and `lost` occurs only after 4+ consecutive frames. No change in risk.
6. **Movement hidden by the burst (preexisting limitation, widened).** Stillness (`STILL_TOLERANCE`, `autoCalibrator.ts:8,48`) is checked only in `framing`/`settling` — during `collecting`, movement already did not reset anything. A `lost` burst can hide a shift: samples before and after movement remain “valid,” and the median accommodates both positions (the majority wins; a bimodal tie falls in the middle). The fix slightly widens this window (previously, the burst would reset). Existing mitigations: median + 65% coverage; acceptable, but documented here as a known limitation — not introduced by the fix.
7. **Camera/video stall** (frames stop arriving — `update()` is not called): `collecting` hangs until the next frame, when the deadline jumps forward and `conclude()` decides based on coverage. Unchanged by the fix; the deadline advances only with frames.
8. **Recovery frames are not wasted.** `lost`→`degraded` on the first good frame, `tracked` on the third (`trackingGate.ts:27-30`): with the fix, recovery `degraded` frames count as valid again as soon as confidence rises — the recovery period contributes samples, rather than only waiting.
9. **The pre-collection reset remains correct.** In `framing`/`settling` there are no samples to protect; keeping `reset()` on `lost` there prevents a stillness anchor from surviving a blackout (the person may have moved during the loss — re-anchoring is correct). Code: `autoCalibrator.ts:27-30`; test “restarts stillness when tracking is lost before collection.”
10. **Confirmed inert interactions outside the scope**: `'calibrating'` exists in the `TrackingState` union but nobody emits it (`protocol.ts:3` — dead state); `minTrackingConfidence` is inert with `numPoses=1` (`pose_landmarker_graph.cc` ~L376-379 — association never has both inputs non-empty at the same time); no new mutable shared state — `calibrator.add()` appends immutable snapshots built from replies (`poseClient.ts:140-146`), with no aliasing.
